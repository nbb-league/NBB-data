How the odds work

  • Rosters, positions, injuries, stats and the NBA schedule come from your ESPN league, pulled at the time shown at the top. Each daily refresh brings in the scores so far and counts only the games left in the week.
  • Where a manager has already set a day's lineup in ESPN, the model uses it. For days not set yet, it assumes the best lineup the roster allows across PG, SG, SF, PF, C, G, F, two G/F, two F/C and three UTIL. A slate with fewer players than slots leaves empty slots.
  • Starting projection is 60% last season's average and 40% ESPN's projection (ESPN's runs low for big double-double players). The preseason number now counts for only about one game, so a player's projection is essentially his average this season (a one-game blip is still tempered), and it leans toward his last 15 games once he has played more than 5.
  • Healthy players play 94% of their games, day-to-day players 60%. Injured reserve counts as out. Lineup protection is assumed, so a starter who sits is replaced by the best eligible bench player who plays that day.
  • Each simulated week draws every player's true level (uncertain), then each game around it. Bigger projections swing more.
